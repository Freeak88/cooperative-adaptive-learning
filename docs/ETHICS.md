# Ethics and epistemic boundaries

## Descriptive ≠ normative

Natural selection, cellular cooperation, game-theoretic incentives and computer model selection are descriptive mechanisms. They do not by themselves justify how people should be treated. An individual's social or moral worth **must not** be reduced to measured productivity, intelligence or cooperation.

## Precautions

- Avoid biological metaphors such as "cancer" or "oxidation" as labels for people or communities.
- Do not infer that synthetic origin alone determines moral standing. The nature and welfare of potentially conscious artificial systems are unsettled research questions.
- Evaluation of computational agents does not imply that their deletion or retention is equivalent to the treatment of humans.
- For human participation, follow applicable research ethics review, informed consent, privacy and safeguarding requirements.
- Protect communities from extraction: credit data and knowledge contributors, publish usable documentation, seek local benefit and use appropriate licenses.
- Document conditions where cooperative behavior benefits insiders while harming outsiders.
- Never use fairness averages to justify severe harms to individual participants.
- Transparent failure reporting matters as much as exciting positive results.

## Research governance

Researchers retain responsibility for study design, benchmark integrity and resource accounting. The system may adapt *policies* within a bounded action set, but may not edit its own success criteria, concealed test sets, ethics guardrails or spend ceilings.

## Relationship to political and biological thought

Kropotkin, Ostrom, ecological theory and multicellular cooperation inform the questions asked; none prove that an AI algorithm is better. Oxidative stress is not identical to cancer or social conflict. We reject the naturalistic fallacy from "evolved" to "morally good".

## Open collaboration

Different disciplines and political perspectives are welcome. Contributions should be evidence-based, attribute sources, disclose limitations and avoid claiming that computational results validate a social ideology.
